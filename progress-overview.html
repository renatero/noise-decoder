What is propaganda?

Propaganda is communication shaped to influence an audience in service of an agenda. It can use true facts, false claims, selective context, emotional language, or a mixture of them. A false statement is not automatically propaganda, and a persuasive phrase alone does not prove that its author intended to manipulate anyone. Context matters.

There is no single universal count of propaganda techniques: taxonomies differ. Noise Decoder uses the 18-technique inventory described by Da San Martino and colleagues in Fine-Grained Analysis of Propaganda in News Articles. The examples below are invented illustrations, not claims about real people or events.

TechniqueIllustrative example
Loaded language“This shameful plan will hurt families.”
Name calling / labeling“Those parasites have no place in public life.”
Repetition“Safety first, safety always, safety for everyone.”
Exaggeration / minimization“Every household will be ruined” or “it is only a tiny cost.”
Doubt“Can we really trust those figures?”
Appeal to fear / prejudice“If this changes, your neighborhood will be in danger.”
Flag waving“Real patriots support this proposal.”
Causal oversimplification“One policy caused the entire downturn.”
Slogans“One people, one future.”

Black-and-white fallacy“Either back this bill or stand against progress.”
Thought-terminating cliché“Case closed. There is nothing more to discuss.”
Bandwagon“Everyone is choosing it, so you should too.”
Reductio ad Hitlerum“That proposal is just what Hitler would have wanted.”

Red herring“Before we discuss the missing funds, let us talk about office renovations.”
Obfuscation, intentional vagueness, confusion“Appropriate measures were undertaken by relevant parties.”

These labels describe rhetorical patterns. The same wording may be ordinary argument, humor, quotation, or accurate reporting in context; a candidate cue is a prompt for review, not a verdict.

How Noise Decoder analyzes evidence

Noise Decoder has a public-source dashboard and a user-triggered browser extension. The dashboard can gather public posts and publisher headlines, including Reddit, Bluesky, and Hacker News. The extension extracts visible post-like items only after a user action. Both paths produce records that can be inspected; source errors and incomplete coverage are reported rather than silently treated as zero results.

  1. Normalize posts and linksText is normalized and tokenized. URLs are canonicalized by removing fragments and common tracking parameters while retaining meaningful query values.
  2. Find repeated contentExact canonical-link matches and near-duplicate text connect posts into evidence groups. Text candidates use Jaccard token overlap of at least 0.8 and at least three unique tokens per post.
  3. Check timing and recurrenceWithin related groups, rolling windows (60 minutes by default) identify bursts across distinct accounts. Account pairs count as repeated evidence only across at least two distinct groups or two bursts.
  4. Build and inspect the actor graphAccounts become nodes and recurring pairs become weighted edges. The Python engine uses NetworkX community discovery and includes supporting examples and evidence in its explanations.
  5. Report possible rhetoricTransparent lexical cues mark possible uses of the 18 techniques. A cue only adjusts a network score when that technique appears across at least two accounts in a repeated-evidence community: +5% per technique, capped at +25%.
  6. Compare the strongest cluster with a null modelThe engine runs 199 stratified actor-label permutations and compares the strongest observed cluster with simulated maximum cluster scores. The resulting p-value is separate from the uncalibrated heuristic score.
Interpretation: shared links, similar wording, timing, a community, or a rhetorical cue can all have ordinary explanations. Neither the score nor the permutation p-value establishes deception, intent, or coordination.

Test overview and results

Automated positive and negative examples exercise both sides of the analysis: repeated cross-account patterns should surface for review, while a one-off viral-news example should not form a repeated-evidence community. The labeled text benchmark below evaluates the rhetorical-cue detector, not source collection or end-to-end intent classification.

Headline result: on the PTC sentence-level diagnostic, 96.88% of sentences with no annotated propaganda span were correctly left unflagged (specificity: 11,288 of 11,652 negative sentences). The corpus contains 371 labeled training articles; this is a diagnostic training-split result, not held-out performance or an official SemEval score.

The detector surfaced 646 candidate spans for review. That is the number of predictions, not 646 verified correct detections: 10 predictions exactly matched a gold span, and six exactly matched both a gold span and technique label. Character overlap gives partial credit when spans differ in their boundaries.

The sentence diagnostic treats each non-empty article-body line as one sentence and derives positive/negative labels from overlapping annotated spans. It is a project-defined diagnostic, not the official SemEval sentence-level task score. Span metrics are also project-defined diagnostics; they are not directly comparable with the task leaderboard.

Reddit and the benchmark: the PTC run uses the same fixed set of labeled news articles and lexical rules whether or not Reddit is available as a live source. Adding Reddit expands collection options, but does not materially change this benchmark result. This test does not measure Reddit coverage or compare different Reddit datasets.

The evaluation uses the PTC v2 / SemEval-2020 Task 11 labeled training partition. The original research describes 18 techniques; the SemEval task evaluates a reduced, merged set of 14 classes. See the PTC v2 dataset, the SemEval-2020 Task 11 paper, and the original taxonomy paper.

What an analysis payload contains

A payload is a structured record of the inputs and the evidence used to produce a report. Dashboard submissions use normalized events at POST /api/analyze; the extension payload also carries the content groups, rolling-window results, and permutation analysis it calculated in the browser.

Payload areaTypical contents
EventsPost ID, platform, actor/account ID when visible, text, timestamp, permalink, outgoing links, and source attribution.
Content evidenceCanonical URLs, similar-text pairs, group IDs, match reasons, similarity values, and representative excerpts.
Activity and graphTime-window bursts, recurring account pairs, graph nodes and weighted edges, and discovered communities.
Analysis and coveragePossible technique cues and offsets, network-only and adjusted heuristic scores, permutation statistics, source status/errors, and limitations.

News articles are carried as background context, not account evidence. Reports retain the source list and partial-coverage details so readers can distinguish “no matches” from “source unavailable.”

Where it falls short

  • Lexical rules miss implicit and context-dependent rhetoric, and can flag ordinary language; the PTC results show especially low recall and exact-match precision.
  • The benchmark uses the training partition, not a held-out set, and its sentence score is derived from line-based annotations.
  • Visible account names may not uniquely identify people. Public-source feeds are incomplete and can change.
  • Shared content and timing can reflect a common news event. Network communities and scores are heuristics, not evidence of intent.
  • Only 199 permutations are run, so p-values are coarse; the topic and time strata are approximate.
